# Process overview

*Agent-assisted draft grounded in the development record. The student must revise this into their own account of direction and acceptance before submission.*

The original idea of teaching Vue became a course about inheriting and changing a frontend. That refinement was already proposed in the student's `IDEA.md` ([40feed1](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-u7663394/commit/40feed1)). It gave the semester a narrower purpose than covering APIs: students must explain where application truth belongs as the consequences of changing it grow. The mobile reader and its editorial desk share article identity; the consultation service asks whether the same architectural judgement transfers to a more demanding domain.

The course examples in the brief informed the teaching structure. Calling Bullshit's syllabus sustains one kind of judgement across distinct subjects, while How to Make Almost Anything connects weekly making to cumulative capability. Here, a week begins with an observable failure: editing changes the wrong record, a stale response wins, or logout leaves another copy of the user. Each build repairs a boundary that the next week needs. This was preferable to twelve documentation summaries because it gives both the sequence and the assessment projects a reason to belong together.

The legacy-first sequence makes migration a comparison rather than a reset. Vuex and component contracts solve problems students have already encountered; Pinia can then be evaluated by responsibility instead of line count. TypeScript arrives when consultation records permit contradictory states. The foundation preserves the SlopU platform and frames Vue 2 as archived maintenance work, with fictional consultation data and simulated payments ([3b9859d](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-u7663394/commit/3b9859d)). `CLAUDE.md` records those stable decisions, including the requirement to teach supporting tools before assessing them.

The original specification was committed red before the course existed ([c8c610d](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-u7663394/commit/c8c610d)); the baseline confirmed four failures. Completing all twelve weeks was necessary before the first green implementation checkpoint, making that foundation larger than the subsequent focused commits. The next checkpoint added an independent technology inventory, date-based readiness checks and a visible teaching map in each brief ([9ff9de8](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-u7663394/commit/9ff9de8)). Deleting a requirement cannot manufacture a pass. Planted date, phase, weight, duplicate-outcome and missing-link faults failed before the output was restored.

Automation deliberately stops short of judging whether a lesson is compelling. Distinct metadata does not prove distinct teaching. The prospective-student review therefore read non-adjacent weeks, assessments, the migration comparison, resources and policies. Browser measurements covered 48 content/deck URLs at both marking sizes, all 168 slide/viewport combinations, search, mobile navigation, no-JavaScript reading and resizing. The review found a phone code block wider than its viewport and a Space key consumed by the slide engine. Both were reproduced and fixed; the harness now records the narrower sizing and keyboard rules ([58687a8](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-u7663394/commit/58687a8)). The same commit contains before/after images and measured reports.

The local build, evidence gate, 26 course assertions and 40 tooling tests pass. That establishes a reviewable implementation, not a guaranteed grade or completed submission. The repository remains private, with pushing reserved for the student. Public deployment and the student's final judgement of the course and this account remain explicit submission responsibilities.
